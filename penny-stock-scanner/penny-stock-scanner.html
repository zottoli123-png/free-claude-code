Sell

Your holdings where your rules say Sell half or Sell all, then stocks you don't own that your rules say to avoid. Based on your Rulebook, not personal advice. Selling at a loss and rebuying within 30 days is a wash sale.

✗ Sell: stocks you hold

Sell all: a red flag, on the Avoid list, or the price fell below your stop (3 ATR under your average cost). Sell half: you're in profit and the stock is overbought, stretched, chasing a volume spike, or at resistance.

✗ Avoid: don't buy

Stocks you don't own that hit a red flag or are on the Avoid list (under $1 with a warning sign).

Watch/Avoid

Heavily traded stocks under $1. None of these are candidates: Nasdaq and NYSE require a $1.00 minimum price, so these companies face deficiency notices, reverse splits or delisting.

◎ Watch

$0.10 to $1 with no red flags on file. Not candidates yet; one that climbs back over $1 can show up on the Buy or Hold tab.

✗ Avoid

At or below $0.10 (Nasdaq moves to delist after 10 straight days there), or a red flag hit: delisting notice, toxic financing, going-concern warning, 2+ reverse splits, promotion, or a volume spike of 5× or more with no research on file.

Monthly income estimate

Rough monthly cash from each approach at the rates below, before taxes. Covered-call fund payouts partly come from giving up gains, not from extra profit.

The baseline to beat

Treasury bills and Treasury money market funds pay a few percent with almost no risk. Any strategy, penny stocks included, has to beat this rate after costs to be worth the risk.

Covered calls trade upside for income

Per the shared report, from June 1986 to July 2026 the Cboe BuyWrite index returned 8.6% a year vs 11.2% for the S&P 500, with lower swings (10.7% vs 15.2% volatility). You collect premium, but give up big rallies and keep most of the downside.

Why not on penny stocks

Covered calls and cash-secured puts need 100 shares per contract and liquid options. Most stocks under $3 have no listed options. Selling puts on a struggling company can mean buying 100 shares just before it collapses.
